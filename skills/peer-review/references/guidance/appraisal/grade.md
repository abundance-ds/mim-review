# GRADE: certainty-of-evidence guide

Selected review guide; not the complete GRADE handbook. Schünemann HJ et al., Cochrane Handbook chapter 14, especially §§14.1.6 and 14.2–14.3. Factual framework restated; source rights retained.

Source: [Official source](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-14).

Assess an outcome-specific body of evidence for a defined comparison, population and time horizon—not a manuscript’s reporting score.

| Domain | Review question |
| --- | --- |
| Risk of bias | Assess limitations of contributing results using appropriate design-specific tools. |
| Inconsistency | Examine unexplained differences in effects, including direction and magnitude. |
| Indirectness | Compare populations, interventions, comparisons and outcomes with the target question. |
| Imprecision | Examine confidence intervals against meaningful decision thresholds and information size. |
| Publication bias | Assess missing studies/results and selective dissemination. |
| Possible upgrading | Where appropriate, consider large effects, dose response, or plausible confounding that would reduce the observed effect. |

Document the starting level and rationale for every change. Avoid double counting, especially when ROBINS-I already incorporates confounding. Report high/moderate/low/very-low certainty, relative and absolute effects where appropriate, study/participant counts, and explanations in the Summary of Findings. Certainty is distinct from recommendation strength; use the full guidance for formal grading.
